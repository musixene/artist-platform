# Private feedback and data controls

Likes record current preference, not listening evidence. Notes to Artist are private plain text visible only to their author and authorized artist administrators; there is no public discussion, reply service or notification delivery. Do not include sensitive information you do not want the artist's administrators to read.

Listeners may like/unlike published tracks and submit multiple immutable notes of up to 2,000 Unicode characters each. Notes have retry identifiers to prevent duplicate submissions after a lost response. Server-side sessions, published-track checks, origin/CSRF validation and a persisted 20-change-per-minute allowance enforce write boundaries. Identical like/note retries do not use that allowance. No client-supplied user ID changes the author.

Unpublishing prevents new likes/notes and media access, but authors retain access to their own existing feedback through **My private feedback** and per-track own-data endpoints. Other listeners cannot read it. Disabling an account retains its data but denies access until re-enabled. Existing administrator bootstrap and last-administrator protections remain in force.

Authors can delete individual notes or all their own feedback, and export their own feedback in private JSON pages of up to 50 notes and 50 likes. Exports include pagination/counts; continue through every page for a full export. Pages reflect current data, not a frozen snapshot. No password/session hashes are exported. Administrators can filter by track/listener, inspect current likes and notes, remove notes without editing author text, and export matching pages. Administrator removals and export actions are audited without note bodies; audit entries are pruned after 90 days on subsequent audit activity. Exports and deletion controls share the persisted feedback rate allowance to bound audit growth. Export files are private and should be protected.

Notes and likes remain until explicitly deleted. Own deletion immediately removes feedback from the live database; other users' feedback and all music remain intact. Deleting an account through an authorized operator workflow must delete its feedback too (foreign keys enforce cascades). This release offers feedback deletion, not self-service deletion of the entire login account. Coordinate full account deletion with the instance operator. Disabled accounts are not automatically deleted.

Instance operators control backups and must publish an appropriate backup expiry schedule and private export handling policy before inviting listeners. Deleted feedback may remain in restricted backups until expiry; a restoration must reconcile intervening deletion requests before reopening access. No finite backup expiry is promised by the software. Do not copy feedback into source control or unrelated development installations. No external tracker, message delivery, or centralized Musixene account is required.

Schema 3 adds feedback tables/indexes, rate state and a minimal privacy audit log in an atomic migration, leaving existing accounts, sessions, catalog, collections and media intact. Back up the complete stopped instance before upgrading. Downgrades require matching code and a complete pre-upgrade restore, not a schema edit or overlay. Version-1 preservation, schema-3 failure/retry and populated feedback archive restore are automated tests. Credits remain disabled. Playback analytics is a separately reviewed follow-up; this release does not collect listening duration.

## Player-first interface

Like and Note to Artist now appear only for the active track in the full player. A note dialog names that track explicitly. If playback changes, the previous draft is kept in signed-in memory for its original track and the dialog switches to the new active track without copying the old text into it. Drafts disappear at sign-out/reload; they are never stored in the shell cache or browser storage. My private feedback is available under Account and privacy. Store/catalog browsing does not interrupt music. Schema 4 preserves existing Likes/notes and their author/access rules; no playback analytics is introduced. Public artist logo/favicon/PWA icon assets are distinct from private track artwork and music.

## Administrator diagnostic recordings

Diagnostics are separate from listener analytics. The operator must permit them, an administrator must enable them for the instance, and that administrator must explicitly Start Debug. Ordinary listeners are never recorded. An active administrator recording can contain track identifiers, playback position/duration and queue state, sanitized browser errors, coarse browser/OS information and application lifecycle timing. It does not contain private notes, credentials, cookies, request bodies or complete user-agent strings.

Events stay in bounded device-side IndexedDB during recording. Stop Debug or logout uploads a completed private report using the original authenticated session; no events are streamed, periodically uploaded or forwarded to Musixene. A recording cannot resume under a fresh login. A failed upload can be retried or exported only within its original session, and logout offers explicit export/discard recovery. Other sessions cannot open the old local report through application controls, including when browser storage could not be cleared.

Only the originating administrator can inspect, export, copy or delete a saved report. This access does not grant other administrators access to it. Default server retention is seven days, configurable from one to thirty days; additional event/byte/report/registration quotas bound storage. Local recording and registration lifetime is at most 24 hours. Deleted reports may remain in restricted backups or operator-managed exports; the operator's backup/export policy applies. Diagnostic records are not proof of listening or a basis for charges. Physical-device investigation results must be shared manually at the artist's discretion. See the [architecture](architecture.md#session-bound-diagnostics-03) and [operator guide](operations/running.md#private-diagnostics-operation-and-upgrade-precautions) for the exact boundaries and limitations.

Phase 2 administrator recordings may also include sanitized same-origin media paths/track IDs, browser-exposed resource timings/status/byte sizes, media buffer/seekable ranges, MIME support hints and overlapping-operation counters. The manual foreground probe records safe response headers from a later authenticated one-byte Range request; it does not consume or retain audio content. Passive recording creates no extra media requests. Arbitrary URLs/query strings, credentials, cookies and auth headers are excluded; later probes are never mislabeled as original failures. Reports remain local until the existing single stop/logout upload. Format 2 is backward compatible with retained format-1 reports; no listener analytics or server request logging is added.

## Qualifying-play analytics and credits (0.4)

The approved analytics policy stores one qualifying play after 60 seconds observed, never skips or continuous progress logs. Private administrator track/day aggregates cover 90 days. Listener account-menu actions export own play/credit data and unlink identity from existing analytics. Aggregate totals and append-only financial audit entries remain; future qualifying plays are recorded. Unlimited eligibility is administrator-controlled and audited. Browser-local pending qualification receipts contain only engagement/owner IDs and expiry (maximum 20, 24 hours); no credentials or audio. See [retention, uncertainty and enforcement details](credit-metering-review.md). Diagnostic recording remains separate and opt-in; qualifying analytics do not enable diagnostics.
